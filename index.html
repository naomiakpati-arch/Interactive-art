It was painted in 1889, while Van Gogh was in an asylum in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, France. The painting depicts the view from the east-facing window of his asylum room at night, although it was painted from memory during the day. It has been in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York City since 1941, acquired through the Lillie P. Bliss Bequest. The Starry Night is regarded as among Van Gogh's finest works and is one of the most recognized monuments in the history of Western culture. The Starry Night Transforms the view from Van Gogh's window into a swirling, dreaming vision of the night sky, framed by the quiet village below and the dark, rolling hills above,

The bright crescent moon and the stars are depicted with halos of light, and the sky is alive with whirling clouds, stars ablaze with their own light, and a bright crescent moon. The painting has been interpreted as a depiction of Van Gogh's turbulent quest for serve and serenity, and as a reflection of his interest into the purpose of capturing the spiritual in nature's beauty. The painting is often seen as a reflection of Van Gogh's inner turmoil and his struggle with mental struggles, but yet created beauty out of that. The Starry Night is a great sense of his creativity

Artistic Techniques

Van Gogh used bold, expressive brushstrokes to create the dynamic movement in the sky. The swirling patterns and vibrant colors convey a sense of energy and emotion.
